# Post and comment structures

Shapes for the texts `engage` drafts: comments on other people's posts, a repost with the candidate's own take, and the one post that pays off during a search. The rules that bind them (40 to 90 words per comment, one confirmed number at most, kind in every line, nothing internal about the current employer) are in `modes/_writing.md` → **Outreach and comment rules**. Every fact comes from `cv.md`, `article-digest.md` or what the candidate says; a number appears only when confirmed.

Adapted from the post structures in a public LinkedIn job-search guide (read 2026-10-19), rewritten for career-ops's fact rules.

## Comment shapes

A comment adds exactly one of these, in two to five sentences:

- **An experience.** What happened when the candidate's team did the thing the post describes, with the outcome in plain words (a number only if confirmed).
- **A counterpoint.** Where it worked differently for the candidate and why, stated with respect for the author's point.
- **A concrete example.** One specific practice that makes the post's advice real: what the team did on a Tuesday, not a principle.
- **A question that moves the thread.** What the candidate genuinely wants to know from the author, after one sentence of their own view, so the question is not a quiz.

Shape of every comment: the first sentence states the candidate's view or experience; the middle sentences give the specific; the last sentence closes on the point or on the question. No "Great post", no restating the post, no emoji strings, no hashtags, no links to the candidate's own material. Written in the language of the post.

## Repost with a take

One to three lines above the reposted post: what the candidate agrees with, what they would add, or how it played out for them. Same rules as a comment, shorter. Never a bare repost: a repost without a line of the candidate's own counts for nothing.

## Open to work post

Only when the candidate asks for it and confirms they can say publicly that they are looking. Written as a pitch to the right reader, not as a plea. Short lines, one idea each, a blank line between blocks, dashes for lists, no wall of hashtags, no paragraph longer than a line and a half. Half to one desktop screen in total.

1. **Hook.** One short line; a confirmed number helps. Offer three candidates for the candidate to choose from.
2. **Who I am.** Three to five lines: title, years of experience from confirmed dates, the domains, the scope (teams, people), one thing that sets the candidate apart.
3. **Three things I am proud of.** Three outcomes, one line each, every one confirmed in `cv.md` or `article-digest.md`. The candidate's own actions and the team's outcomes stay distinct.
4. **What I am looking for.** Role titles, product type, company stage, location and workplace, in the candidate's own words from `modes/_context.md` and `config/profile.yml` → `interests` (with the staleness rule in `modes/_writing.md` → **Interests in candidate-facing text**).
5. **How I work.** Two to four lines on the candidate's approach, in their words, never from a template.
6. **Call to action.** One line on what a reader can do: a message, an introduction, a role to point the candidate to.
7. **Call for reach.** One line asking for a like or a comment so the right people see it.

Attach nothing, or one image (a photo, not the CV). The CV goes to whoever asks for it. The candidate approves the draft before it is used anywhere.

## Post about the search (optional, only with real data)

A post about the search reads best as an experiment with numbers: applications sent, replies, interviews, what changed the ratio. Only from the candidate's own tracker (`data/applications.md` and `node tracker.mjs stats`), never invented, never with company names attached to rejections.

## Post about what the candidate knows (long-term)

One practice the candidate has run, as a post: the problem, what the team did, what happened, what they would do differently. One post, one practice. The same truth rules as a CV.

## Format rules (all posts)

- The first line decides whether anyone reads on; make it specific.
- Lines of at most one and a half desktop lines, a blank line between them.
- One useful thing the reader can apply today.
- An image, a carousel or a poll raises reach.
- One language per post: the audience's. LinkedIn translates.
- To shorten a draft without losing the point, ask for it shorter while keeping the main idea.
