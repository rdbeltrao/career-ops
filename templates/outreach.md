# Outreach structures

Paragraph roles for the messages `contacto` and the referral variant of `email` draft, in a fixed order. The text under each role says what that paragraph does; it is never text to copy, and no example here is anyone's fact. The rules that bind every message (length, one confirmed number, honest reason before facts, never a job ask) are in `modes/_writing.md` → **Outreach and comment rules**.

Adapted from the message structures in a public LinkedIn job-search guide (read 2026-10-19), rewritten for career-ops's fact rules.

## Recruiter or hiring manager

An opening exists and the candidate writes to the recruiter or the hiring manager. Goes as a LinkedIn message after connecting, or as an InMail; the CV and the cover letter attach only when the channel allows it.

1. **Greeting.** First name only.
2. **Who the candidate is.** Name and current role in one sentence, in the wording `cv.md` uses.
3. **Interest.** The role title, where the candidate saw it, and one honest reason it fits (the team, the product, the way of working). No "perfect match".
4. **Hook.** One paragraph of what in the candidate's experience matches this opening: two or three concrete facts from `cv.md` or `article-digest.md`, at most one number. This is the only paragraph that may carry a number.
5. **Why this company.** One paragraph on what caught the candidate's attention, from the posting or the company's own pages. Specific, not "your culture".
6. **The ask.** A short conversation about the role, or whether the application reached the right hands. Mention the CV only when the channel lets the candidate attach it.
7. **Close.** One line of thanks, then the candidate's first name.

## Referral

An opening exists and the person works at the company but has no prior relationship with the candidate. (A contact with a real prior relationship is `contacto`'s Internal Referral persona, which asks directly.)

1. **Greeting.**
2. **Who the candidate is.**
3. **Why this person.** One paragraph on why the candidate writes to them in particular, only from what the candidate stated: their path at the company, a post of theirs the candidate read, a shared employer or school, a mutual connection. When nothing is known, skip the paragraph and say plainly that the candidate found them on the company's team page.
4. **Interest.** The opening by title, and one honest reason it fits.
5. **Hook.** Two or three matching facts, at most one number.
6. **The ask.** One question: could they refer the candidate, pass the CV to the right person, or say who owns the hiring. Name what is attached.
7. **Close.**

## Networking

No opening. The candidate wants to know someone relevant: a peer with the target title, a manager one level above it, someone in a domain the candidate is moving toward.

1. **Greeting.**
2. **Who the candidate is.** Name and field in one sentence.
3. **Why this person.** A post of theirs, a shared context, or that the candidate has followed their work, and what it made the candidate think. Only what the candidate states.
4. **Hook.** One paragraph of what the candidate does: two or three facts, at most one number.
5. **The ask plus what the candidate offers.** A short call to hear about the person's experience with one named topic, and what the candidate brings to it: a comparison from their own setup, an introduction, feedback on something the person is building.
6. **Call to action with a choice.** Two concrete options for when, so the reply is a pick rather than a plan.
7. **Close.** Thanks and first name.

## Connection note

The note on a connection invitation. Only when there is something real to say; a "nice to meet you" note is worse than no note, so with nothing real the invitation goes without one.

1. **Who the candidate is**, in half a sentence.
2. **The one thing that made the candidate click Connect**: a post, a shared company, the role the person hires for.

No ask in the note. One or two sentences, under 300 characters, and under 200 on a free account (`contacto` → Message rules). The ask comes in the message after they accept, built from one of the structures above.

## Length and tone

- Message: 90 to 140 words. Note: under the character limit above.
- Short paragraphs with a blank line between them. LinkedIn renders no formatting, so no bold, no bullets.
- Plain first person. The reader should hear a person, not a cover letter.
- One number at most, confirmed. No lists of tools, no stacked metrics.
- English unless the candidate says otherwise; the language of the posting when it is not English and the candidate writes it.
